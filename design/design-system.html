Premium isn't more decoration. It's a lot of small, unseen details that are all right together. Each change below comes from a specific rule.

Principles

The feeling is calm confidence.

Apple's advice is to pick one emotion and back it up in every decision. Ours is calm. When two principles conflict, the one higher up wins.

01

The glyph says what you hear. The text says why.

Full, low or flat bars show the sound. "Paused for Zoom" shows the cause. Mauna never goes quiet without a reason on screen.

02

Part of macOS

System font, system materials, native controls tinted with one accent. When something is right, nobody notices it.

03

Motion only when it means something

Every animation has a reason: state, feedback or where something came from. Things people use often stay instant, and nothing loops.

04

Handle edge cases quietly

Debounce, override, short sounds, two rules at once. The best edge-case handling is the one nobody ever sees.

Colour

One accent, used rarely.

Champagne gold, from the black-and-gold icon, means one thing: Mauna is acting right now. Everything else is a slightly tinted neutral from macOS semantic colours, so light, dark and increased contrast all work without extra effort.

Depth

Rings and shadows, not borders.

Three elevation levels. Bigger surfaces sit higher and cast deeper shadows. We never stack one translucent layer on another, because text stops being readable.

e1 · rows, chips, controls
e2 · cards, settings groups
e3 · popover, windows

In SwiftUI the popover and settings window get their material and shadow from the system. These levels only apply to our own surfaces, like the onboarding cards and the YouTube Music setup sheet.

Motion

Decide whether it should animate at all.

For every Mauna moment, we ask how often people see it and why it moves. The real animation in Mauna is the audio fade. The interface just keeps up with it.

Press

Responds on press, not on release. .buttonStyle with scaleEffect(0.97) and .snappy.

Spring morph

Click quickly to interrupt it. It always picks up from where it is now. In SwiftUI: .spring(response: 0.3, dampingFraction: 1).

Playing on Spotify

Blur crossfade

Text changes go through a 2px blur so you never see two words on top of each other. .contentTransition(.opacity) plus .blur.

Audio fade

Equal-power curves: fading out sounds even, and fading in starts gently. A slower return feels kinder. Each fade starts from the volume playing right now, so a fade that gets interrupted never jumps.

Icon · Concept B

The quiet wave.

Five bars that settle as the music steps aside. We build it as a custom SF Symbol, so macOS tints it in the menu bar and SwiftUI can morph between states with .contentTransition(.symbolEffect(.replace)).

The same glyph means the same sound. Low bars cover both "Quieted by you" and "Lowered for Xcode", and the status line says which. Off is shown at reduced opacity, the way macOS shows disabled menu bar items.

App icon direction

Sound stepping aside from left to right, on a dusk gradient with light from the top left. This is a flat sketch. The final icon gets layered glass in Icon Composer: the bars on a front layer, the gradient behind, and the system's specular edge on top.

Voice

Short, plain, specific.

Name the app, then the next step. Never blame the user.

Say
  • Paused for Zoom · Resumes when the call ends
  • Quieted by you · Press ⌃⌥Space to bring it back
  • Still paused for Zoom
  • So Mauna can pause and play Spotify.
  • Can't reach Chrome. Allow JavaScript from Apple Events.
Don't say
  • Auto-pause engaged!
  • Mauna needs access to continue
  • Error: AppleEvent −1743
  • Exclamation marks, emoji, "Oops"
  • Ducked (in status text). Use "Lowered".
